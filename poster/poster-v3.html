01Background & research gap

Battery RUL research has four mature ingredients — but no published framework combines them. Each thread solves one piece and stops there; a maintenance decision needs all four at once.

The gap we fill: verified against a systematic review of 33 works — the closest LSTM+filter hybrid [28] explicitly lists our physics constraints as future work; GPU particle filters [17] never touched battery data. P3-EVHM closes all four cells at once, on real NASA PCoE cells, across two machines.

02This work — the breakthrough in three contributions

C1 — Physics-informed training that removes impossible predictions. Two electrochemistry laws enter the loss (Eq. 4–5): capacity may never rise, and decline must respect an Arrhenius-style temperature prior. Implausible (rising-capacity) steps fall 0.366 → 0.076 — a ~5× cut, with better capacity MAE (0.285 → 0.257 Ah).

C2 — RUL as a calibrated distribution, not a guess. The trained model seeds thousands of simulated futures (Eq. 6–8) reweighted by the measured capacity; we report the median, the 95% interval, and decision rules driven by its lower bound: a RUL estimate in 20/20 configurations on both machines (mean error 18.8 cycles, interval coverage 0.85).

C3 — GPU-scale particle inference, open and reproducible. The cloud updates as one vectorised tensor op per step (SIMT): 16.5× end-to-end at 500,000 particles, sub-100 ms device time throughout — shipped as an MIT framework (pip, dashboard, CI, Kaggle kernel) with 40 measured configurations.

A 1–32 LSTM over a W = 8 window; identical seeds and metrics for every baseline B1–B6 so the comparison is the physics, not the tuning.

04System — five agents, one framework

A3 and A4 run on the GPU; the same code runs on CPU for parity. Four doors into the same engine: Python API, CLI, dashboard, Kaggle kernel.

05Evidence 1 — the physics loss works

Violation rate V per configuration (share of predicted steps where capacity rises — physically impossible). Every one of 20 configurations improves or ties; the two catastrophes (V = 1.0, configs 04/08) are eliminated entirely.

07Evidence 3 — GPU scaling

Honest crossover reported: below ~5k particles the warm CPU wins (0.65×); the GPU pays exactly where prognostics needs it — large clouds, sharp intervals, real time.

08Conclusions & future work

Conclusions. The fusion is the contribution: physics constraints make LSTM prognostics physically consistent at no accuracy cost; the particle filter turns that forecast into a calibrated maintenance decision in every configuration tested; and GPU-scale inference makes the cloud size that sharp intervals need affordable. Open-sourced end to end, with 40 measured configurations as evidence.

Future work. Pack-level validation beyond single cells; prior-floor tuning for early-start predictions (40% of life); interval coverage toward 0.95; streaming on-vehicle deployment.
