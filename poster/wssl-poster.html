Parallel Physics-Informed Prognostics for EV Battery Health Management (P3-EVHM): Fusing Physics Constraints, Calibrated RUL and GPU-Scale Inference
A. Vibhas Chowdhary, Maddala Jithendra  —  guide: Dr. R. Kavitha
va1641@srmist.edu.in · jm4408@srmist.edu.in · kavithar14@srmist.edu.in

Abstract

Battery remaining-useful-life (RUL) research has mature ingredients that no published framework combines: physics-informed learning, calibrated uncertainty, GPU-scale inference and a real-cell benchmark. This work fuses all four. A physics-informed LSTM — trained with a monotonicity penalty and an Arrhenius-style temperature prior — cuts physically implausible predictions from 0.366 to 0.076 mean violation rate. A particle filter turns the forecast into a distribution: RUL in 20/20 configurations on two machines (mean error 18.8 cycles, coverage 0.85). The cloud runs as one vectorised tensor operation: 16.5× GPU speedup at 500,000 particles. Open-sourced with 40 measured configurations and full provenance.

1 Introduction

Lithium-ion cells lose capacity with every cycle — slowly, then through a degradation knee that crosses the 70–80% end-of-life threshold with almost no warning. A vehicle reports charge percentage, not health. The central question: how many cycles remain — and with what confidence — before the battery must be replaced? Maintenance needs a calibrated number early enough to schedule replacement, not a point estimate at the cliff.

3 Validation protocol

  • Data: NASA Ames PCoE cells B0005/B0006/B0007/B0018 [1], byte-verified
  • Grid: 40 configurations — 4 cells × 5 starts × EOL 70/80%, on a Core Ultra CPU and a Kaggle Tesla T4
  • Baselines B1–B6: linear, LSTM, PI-LSTM, LSTM+PF, physics+PF, fused P3-EVHM — same seeds, same metrics

4 The P3-EVHM model

A 1–32 LSTM over a W = 8 cycle window; the loss carries two electrochemistry laws:

(4) L = MSE(q̂, q) + λ1·mean ReLU(q̂t − qt) + λ2·mean(Δq̂t − g(Tt))²
(5) g(T) = clamp(Â(T − T̄), min = 0)

Capacity may never rise; decline must respect a temperature prior fitted from the training cycles themselves. Full-batch training, deterministic seeds, identical metrics for every baseline.

5 Particle-filter RUL

(6) qi(k) = qi(k−1) − di + εk
(7) wi ∝ exp(−(qobs − qi)²/2σ²)
(8) RUL = median { k : qi(k) ≤ qEOL }

Thousands of simulated futures, reweighted by the measured start-cycle capacity; each future’s threshold crossing is a RUL draw. We report the median and 95% interval — and the decision rules (OK / CAUTION / ALERT) follow the interval’s lower bound.

6 Physics-consistency results

Violation rate V per configuration (predicted steps where capacity rises — impossible). All 20 configurations improve or tie; the two catastrophes (V = 1.0) are eliminated. Capacity MAE also improves: 0.285 → 0.257 Ah.

8 GPU scaling

Honest crossover: below ~5k particles the warm CPU wins (0.65×); the GPU pays exactly where prognostics needs it — large clouds, sharp intervals, real time (20.2 ms device time at 500k).

9 Main findings

  1. Physics constraints work. Implausible predictions fall ~5× (0.366 → 0.076), with better capacity MAE on the same grid.
  2. Uncertainty is deliverable. Calibrated RUL in every configuration, both machines; from-scratch training failed 3 of 4 transfer cells.
  3. GPU scaling is workload-dependent. Parity at small N; 16.5× at 500k particles, <100 ms device time throughout.
  4. Transfer beats retraining. 0.2 s head-only adaptation: 4/4 RUL availability, zero violations.

10 Conclusion

Reliable battery prognostics is simultaneously a physics and an HPC problem. The fusion is the contribution: combining the parts turns LSTM outputs into maintenance-grade decisions. The optimization target is a trustworthy RUL distribution in production time — not a point estimate.

11 Limitations

  • Laboratory single-cell data (NASA PCoE); pack-level validity untested
  • Early starts (40% of life) can fail without the decline-prior floor; LSTM seed variance is material and reported
  • Research decision-support prototype — not a certified BMS safety function
